Governance

A structure and responsibilities defined by the association’s statutes.

Smart YIRIBA’s governance rests on three bodies defined in its statutes: the General Assembly, the Executive Board and the Advisory Committee.

The statutes of 15 May 2018 define the bodies and roles presented here. This page does not identify the current holders of the other board roles.

Three bodies with defined responsibilities

01 · Guide and oversee

General Assembly

Composed of the association’s members, the General Assembly is the supreme and sovereign body. It provides strategic direction and oversees activities and compliance with procedures and internal regulations.

  • Meets at least once a year in an ordinary session.
  • Deliberates on decisions proposed by the Executive Board and adopts internal regulations.
  • Reviews the annual activity and financial report and approves the accounts for the completed financial year.
  • Elects the members of the Executive Board (Article 18).

Articles 11 and 13

02 · Manage and implement

Executive Board

As the executive body, the board handles Smart YIRIBA’s day-to-day management and implements its activities and programmes. Board positions are in principle voluntary.

  • Sets the action plan and budget.
  • Accepts donations and bequests and authorises the operations specified in the statutes outside routine management.
  • Sets the conditions for recruiting and remunerating programme staff.
  • Establishes programme directorates with operational autonomy under its responsibility.

Articles 11, 12 and 14

03 · Analyse and advise

Advisory Committee

On request, the Advisory Committee provides analyses, reflections and proposals for actions and activities.

  • Meets as needed.
  • Is convened by the Executive Board.

Articles 11 and 15

Statutory composition of the board

Under Article 18, the General Assembly elects a six-member board. Article 14 sets out the responsibilities of the President, Secretary General and Treasurer.

President

Represents the association in civil matters and authorises expenditure.

Secretary General

Handles general administration and replaces the President in their absence.

Deputy Secretary General

A position included in the board composition under Article 18.

Communications Officer

A position included in the board composition under Article 18.

Organising Secretary

A position included in the board composition under Article 18.

Treasurer

Collects income and pays expenses.

Decisions, elections and transparency

Elections

The President is elected by vote and the General Assembly elects the board. Voters must be members of the General Assembly and up to date with their dues. Eligibility criteria are set out in Article 17.

Articles 16 to 18

Extraordinary assemblies

An extraordinary assembly may be convened at the President’s initiative or at the request of at least two thirds of the General Assembly’s members.

Article 13

Amending the statutes

Amendments to the statutes require a General Assembly decision by a two-thirds majority of active members present. Internal regulations supplement the statutory provisions.

Articles 19 and 21

Founding members

Presidency and institutional representation

Association presidency

Aboul Hassane CISSE

Executive President
Representative of the Association in civil matters

A founding member, he signed the statutes of 15 May 2018 as Executive President. Article 14 also makes the President responsible for authorising expenditure.

Transparency and funding

Article 10 of the statutes provides for the following sources of funding:

  • Membership dues
  • Income from programmes and services
  • Grants
  • Donations
  • Bequests

The General Assembly examines the activity and financial report and approves the annual accounts (Article 13). No financial report or annual accounts are currently published on the website.
